Who we are

A farm that grows where food is needed most

Nuru Agribusiness Farm is based in Southern Luri, Juba, Central Equatoria, South Sudan. The farm produces vegetables, pumpkins, goats, and premium fruits on a phased development of 36 to 50 acres — growing food close to the people who eat it and cutting out the costly import supply chain.

Nuru means light. The name reflects the farm's purpose: to bring reliable food supply, employment, and economic opportunity to the Luri area.

What we grow

Four product lines. One farm.

🥬
18 acres · 2–3 seasons/year

Vegetables

The core of the farm — 18 acres of diversified vegetable production across two to three growing seasons per year. Crops are chosen for consistent local demand and climate resilience. Tomatoes, onions, and leafy greens form the high-volume lines; okra, cabbage, eggplant, and carrots add diversity and market spread.

Tomatoes Onions Carrots Kale & Spinach Okra Cabbage Eggplant
🎃
10 acres · 80–100 t/year

Pumpkins

Ten acres of mixed pumpkin varieties producing 80–100 tons annually. Pumpkins are a South Sudan staple, require less water than most vegetables, and tolerate seasonal variation. Leaves are also harvested and sold — providing additional revenue at zero extra input cost.

Mixed varieties Leaves sold Drought tolerant
🐉
Premium · High-value exports

Premium Fruits

Dragon fruit and avocado represent Nuru's premium and export-oriented product line. Both thrive in South Sudan's climate and command significantly higher market prices than staple crops, targeting urban buyers, hotels, and regional export markets.

Dragon Fruit Avocado High-value Export potential
🐐
Phase 1: 60 head · Phase 2: 100

Goats

A herd of 60 goats in Phase 1, growing to 100 in Phase 2. Managed primarily for meat, with does also providing milk for local sale. Breeds are selected for climate resilience and feed efficiency, with a structured veterinary program.

Meat Milk 60 → 100 head

Our advantage

Why Nuru delivers where imports cannot

📍
Local production
Growing in Luri eliminates 500–1,000 km of import transport. Produce reaches buyers fresher, at lower delivered cost, and with more reliable supply.
🌦️
Climate-suited location
Southern Luri's rainfall, soil, and temperature conditions support year-round vegetable production, pumpkin cultivation, and livestock rearing.
💧
Irrigation independence
A drilled borehole and irrigation system reduce dependence on seasonal rainfall, supporting consistent supply through dry periods.
🤝
Multiple buyer channels
Nuru supplies local market traders, household farm gate sales, NGO food programs, hotels, and regional traders — spreading market risk across channels.
📈
Phased, capital-efficient growth
Phase 1 at 36 acres establishes the operation. Phase 2 scales to 50 acres using revenue generated, without requiring fresh capital injection.

Development roadmap

Three phases. Steady expansion.

Phase 01

Foundation

Year 1 – Year 2

Land preparation, borehole drilling, irrigation installation, storage construction, goat pen, first planting cycle (vegetables, pumpkins), goat herd acquisition. Market entry and first revenues.

36 ac
Phase 02

Expansion

Year 3

Add 14 acres, extend irrigation, grow goat herd to 100, hire a dedicated farm manager. Begin premium fruit cultivation (dragon fruit, avocado). Scale institutional buyer relationships.

50 ac
Phase 03

Scale & Process

Year 4+

Food processing capability, premium fruit export channels, agricultural training programs for surrounding communities, and supply chain partnerships with regional buyers and NGOs.

50+ ac
